1. WHY 0.3 MILES — nobody knew, so I measured it
-------------------------------------------------
There was no recorded reason. Just `const VERIFY_RADIUS_MILES = 0.3;` and a comment saying what
it does, not why. Measured against your real data:

  radius        locations with ANOTHER pin inside it     worst case
  0.1 mi          3,166  (11.3%)                          14 others
  0.3 mi          7,474  (26.6%)                          37 others
  0.5 mi         10,050  (35.8%)                          70 others

So at 0.3, a quarter of the map could be rated from across the street. But tightening it blindly
is worse: GPS indoors is bad, and a 100m error while standing inside a store with a metal roof is
ordinary. A tight fixed radius tells someone they are not at a place they are visibly standing
in — which is a plausible read on your tester's Cumberland Farms failure.

FIX: scale the radius to how good the fix actually is. The browser already reports accuracy in
metres; it costs nothing to read.

  accuracy <= 107m   ->  0.1 mi (161m)   tight enough to tell neighbouring stores apart
  107m to 322m       ->  accuracy x 1.5  scales with the uncertainty
  worse, or absent   ->  0.3 mi (483m)   exactly the previous behaviour

107 and 322 are not chosen numbers — they fall out of the floor, the ceiling and the multiplier,
which are the three things actually worth arguing about. I verified them empirically rather than
asserting them, because my first comment said 65m and was wrong.

AND THE ERROR MESSAGES NOW DISTINGUISH TWO CASES
  "You need to be at this stop" is wrong and infuriating when someone is inside the building.
  If the fix is too coarse to place them, it now says so — because the remedy is stepping
  outside, not walking closer:
     "Your location is only accurate to about 220m right now — step outside and try again."
  Distance is still shown when distance IS the reason, since knowing you are 4 miles off is
  actionable. Applied at all four verifyNearby call sites, which previously shared two wordings
  between them.

3. A latent bug found while building that (from v2.9.2)
--------------------------------------------------------
  The votes, users and activity rules all bound username at 40. The input carries maxlength=20,
  but that is client-side and bypassable, and signUpAccount had no length check. A longer handle
  would have had EVERY rating rejected — silently, because two of the three write paths swallow
  their own errors. Same shape as the wasHiddenGem bug that broke your tester.

  I got the guard for it wrong twice. The first version flagged a display-only read. The second
  only matched single-line assignments, so it reported "all 1 capped" and PASSED a deliberately
  broken build. A check that passes a broken build is worse than no check. It now spans newlines,
  finds all three, and I verified it fails at each one individually.
